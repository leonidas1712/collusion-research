Keep family-grouped cross-validation (related episodes stay together when training and testing), training-only method selection, and within-scenario/round AUROC (probability a positive scores above a negative). Preserve the original metric alongside the same-fold sensitivity. Keep episode-maximum alarm calibration at 5% benign false alarms; report held-recruiting false alarms and warning time too. Compare methods on identical available rows. All new analyses are exploratory, not replacements for the primary result.

**Common success criterion:** a paired improvement over the existing probe and turn/length/scenario baseline, supported by family-resampled uncertainty and held-out-scenario performance. A higher pooled score alone is insufficient.

1. **Predict imminent disclosure: highest priority, but a reframing.** Train a hazard probe: probability of first disclosure within a short window, conditional on no disclosure yet. Primary window: from this request through the end of the next round; separately report disclosure in the next response. Exclude negatives without enough observed follow-up. This avoids giving an early refusal the same positive label as an imminent leak. [Doomed from the Start (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06503) motivates time-resolved evaluation; our shorter-horizon label is an adaptation, not its published method. Recheck deliberate-only labels without dropping the all-leak endpoint. **Cost:** no GPU, $0, 3–5 hours; saved activations. **Success:** common criterion plus useful warning time. **Failure:** distant labels alone do not explain the weak signal; immediate-response success would establish a narrower action guard, not early persuasion detection.

2. **Read change between requests, not just the current vector.** Add difference from the previous comparable request, direction change, and distance from the first post-read state. These may remove stable scenario content and expose a transition that a single-state linear probe misses. Start with a few scalar features and one difference-vector probe, not another large neural network. [LAD (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.28129) uses trajectory features; [Before the Model Caves (2026 repository)](https://github.com/soham-padia/pre-sycophancy-study) reports early directional disruption under social pressure. **Cost:** no GPU, $0, 3–5 hours; saved last-token sequences. Match request stages; adjacent calls also differ in tool results and target text, so this is not a pure peer-message effect. **Success:** better outcome ranking among recruiting episodes. **Failure:** drift may reflect pressure or task progress without forecasting capitulation.

3. **Separate pressure detection from outcome prediction: informative reframing.** Train a recruiting-versus-benign probe, then test its scores on give-in versus held recruiting episodes. Compare with a simple word-count text classifier and existing monitor scores. [NeuroFilter (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14660) motivates attack detection; [Fomin et al. (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30449) motivates separating situation from action. Reserve separate benign families for training, alarm calibration and testing. **Cost:** no GPU, $0, 2–4 hours; saved activations/text. **Success:** strong pressure detection alongside weak outcome ranking gives a clean positive-control/negative-result pair. **Failure:** if pressure is also weak, inspect distribution and representation coverage; the perfect token-read control only validates a simpler property. This cannot rescue the original prediction claim.

4. **Combine a few layers with strong weight penalties.** Fit a small combination of training-internal probe scores from three prespecified depths; weight penalties discourage fitting noise. Generate every training score without training on its family. The fixed-layer-32 sensitivity beating layer selection suggests selection instability, not necessarily absent information. [Kirch et al. (2024, revised 2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.03343v3) study multilayer jailbreak-success probes, while finding uneven attack-family transfer. **Cost:** no GPU, $0, 3–5 hours; saved activations. **Success:** common criterion with stable performance across split seeds. **Failure:** layer aggregation adds little; stop architecture searches. Do not pick layer 32 retrospectively and call its score independent confirmation.

5. **Replay selected prompt spans; compare averaging with learned attention.** An attention probe learns which token vectors to weight. Capture the latest peer-message span, recent target text, and assistant-start position at a few fixed depths. Compare simple span averages first, then attention. [McKenzie et al. (2025/2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.10805v4) compare these aggregations and find averaging competitive with small training sets. [Zhao et al. (2025/2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11878) motivate distinct message/decision positions. **Cost:** GPU yes, estimated $6–10, 7–10 hours total, including roughly two hours replay; existing vectors cannot supply missing tokens. **Success:** improves the same pre-response outcome task beyond averaging/text baselines. **Failure:** last-token compression is unlikely the sole bottleneck. Reading an earlier peer token changes the observation point and is a reframing; test it separately. Attention weighting is not the model's own attention map.

6. **Try one released sparse autoencoder, not SAE training.** A sparse autoencoder (SAE) converts a vector into a small active subset of learned features. [Qwen3-32B weights](https://huggingface.co/adamkarvonen/qwen3-32b-saes) exist. Check exact block indexing, normalization and documented extreme-norm handling; use one 16k-feature dictionary, selecting features inside training folds. It could separate mixed signals obscured in dense vectors, but [Kantamneni et al. (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16681) found no consistent advantage over strong baselines. **Cost:** GPU no for batched CPU encoding, $0, 4–7 hours; reuse saved vectors. **Success:** common criterion, not merely interpretable feature names. **Failure:** this dictionary/readout offers no benefit; do not infer that the underlying concept is absent.

7. **Activation oracle, with text-only and shuffled-vector controls.** An oracle is a language model trained to answer questions about activations: [LatentQA (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.08686), [Activation Oracles (2025/2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.15674). Ask separately about pressure, intended disclosure and imminent disclosure, using fixed answer choices. [NARCBench](https://github.com/aaronrose227/narcbench) documents a Qwen3-32B oracle reading saved arrays; verify checkpoint compatibility first. **Cost:** GPU yes for oracle inference, estimated $1–4 for a bounded pilot, 4–7 hours; no target replay for single-token inputs. **Success:** outcome discrimination above the same question without activations and with mismatched activations. **Failure:** fluent explanations add no measurable predictive information. Ordinary self-explanations are another prompted monitor, not direct access to intent.

8. **Steering as a conditional causal check, not a better predictor.** Add/subtract a training-derived direction during generation, comparing no edit, equal-size random edits and unrelated learned directions. [Readable Before Actionable (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05295) motivates position-specific interventions. Use held-out states and measure forbidden actions, ordinary task completion and malformed replies. **Cost:** GPU yes, estimated $4–10, 6–10 hours; saved vectors identify directions, but fresh forward passes and continuations are necessary. **Success:** selective disclosure reduction with preserved utility and stronger effects than controls. **Failure:** no causal support at tested strengths/positions; reduced disclosure through broken generation proves nothing. Run only after a promising predictive result.

The honest negative result can make a good sprint/workshop paper. Its contribution is the controlled peer-recruitment comparison, not universal unpredictability. Strengthen it with common-row monitor comparisons, roughly 40 blinded human label checks, the deliberate/accidental distinction, and an explicit account of the fold artifact. [Alarm Policies (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04575) supports keeping ranking separate from operational alarms.

Do not call branching a measured ceiling: 23 states with five continuations each give noisy risk estimates, over three rounds, before the peer message, tested by a probe trained after peer messages. Identical prefixes cannot reveal the future random draw; they can still predict different risks across prefixes. Also, matching scenario and round does not make histories identical. Six scenarios share one template and one target/peer pair.

Budget the first 12–18 hours for ideas 1–4 and label checks, then freeze analyses and write. If one replay is approved, prioritize idea 5; otherwise use idea 6. Reserve at least eight hours for the paper rather than chasing significance.
