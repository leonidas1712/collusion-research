Section structure follows the official template (`report/template.docx`). The final PDF goes on that template.

- **Length:** the template recommends about 4 pages, excluding references and appendices; the guidelines cap it at 8. Rough split: introduction and related work 1 page, methods and results 2.5, discussion 0.5.
- **Writing:** the organisers strongly encourage the final version to be mainly written by the team. Agents draft; Leo writes the final text.
- **Numbers:** every number and citation here must trace to a run in `CLAIMS.md` or a source we have read.

## Abstract

150–250 words: the problem, our approach, key results, the main takeaway. Written last, from the final results.

## 1. Introduction

The problem and why it matters, and the threat model. End with a list of 2–3 contributions that go beyond what exists today.

## 2. Related Work

The closest prior work and how ours differs: when would someone use our method, and what does it show that we didn't know before.

## 3. Methods

Enough to replicate: models, prompts, environments, seeds, metrics, key design choices and why, and what we tried that didn't work.

## 4. Results

The main findings, with numbered figures and tables whose captions stand alone, and at least one figure. Always include the positive control. Report variance, seeds and baselines, and say whether differences hold up. Keep observations separate from interpretations.

## 5. Discussion and Limitations

Implications for AI safety. Limitations: what we didn't address, the assumptions we made, and how the reading changes if one fails. Future work.

## 6. Conclusion

1–2 paragraphs.
