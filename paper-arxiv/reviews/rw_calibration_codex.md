## Calibration for this paper

1. The typical bar in this set is multiple models or multiple environments, an explicit held-out split, and at least one obvious shortcut baseline. Only one of the nine works reports a comprehensive preregistered and hash-locked programme (Fayyazi et al.); several report no confidence interval for the headline probe comparison.
2. A matched **trained surface-text classifier** is not standard. Fayyazi et al. provide the clearest same-information supervised text baseline. Goldowsky-Dill et al. use a minimally tuned GPT-4o transcript monitor, and Advani et al. use a matched trained one-hot action baseline. The other works rely on zero-shot monitors, self-report, structural features, random directions or no text comparator.
3. A **file-action confound check** is not standard in this literature because most studies do not predict file writes. The nearest analogues are Advani et al.'s matched current-tool classifier, Fayyazi et al.'s authorised twin of the same critical action, Yu et al.'s structural and position controls, and MacDiarmid et al.'s coding/non-coding trigger crossing.
4. Our paper is above the typical bar on temporal alignment and comparison fairness: the probe and text monitor score the same model calls, all non-leaking calls are also under peer pressure, and keyword and just-read flags are reported. The fresh gpt-oss test and the preregistered Qwen near-term test are also stronger than the mostly exploratory evaluation in this set.
5. Our paper is at the stronger end for uncertainty reporting: family-grouped 95% bootstrap intervals and held-out-scenario evaluation are at least as careful as most of these works. Its six-scenario rotation is narrower than Rose et al.'s domain transfer, Dong et al.'s strict suite/attack/instruction transfer, or Fayyazi et al.'s fresh matched worlds.
6. Our paper is below the best confound controls for action prediction. It does not yet include a trained text classifier, and it does not test a baseline that knows only whether the next action is a file write. That missing baseline is material for gpt-oss because nearly every first leak is a handoff-file write.
7. Our paper is also below the strongest causal and breadth evidence: two target models and one scenario template are narrower than the 3-8 model studies, and the current manuscript reports no causal intervention on the probe direction. Fayyazi et al., Yu et al. and Kirch et al. pair decoding with behavioural intervention.
8. The defensible related-work claim is therefore narrow: same-call activation probes beat the tested untuned text monitor in this recruitment setting, with shortcut checks and one fresh confirmation. The review does not support a general claim that activation probes beat trained text classifiers, that the probe has isolated intent from imminent file action, or that its direction is causal.
